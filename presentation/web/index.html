01 · What Is Nowcasting?

Atmospheric Prediction on Minute-to-Hour Timescales

Nowcasting is spatially detailed analysis of the current atmosphere and prediction over the immediate future, conventionally from the present to six hours ahead.

Rapidly updated satellite, radar, lightning, and in-situ observations constrain the initial state. Methods include Lagrangian advection, statistical and machine-learning prediction, and blending with numerical weather prediction.

Convective initiation, growth, and dissipation cannot be captured by advection alone. Forecast uncertainty increases as atmospheric structures evolve.

03 · Earth Observations

Observing Europe's atmosphere from space

The forecast begins approximately 36,000 km above Earth.

EUMETSAT's Meteosat-12 observes Europe and surrounding regions using the Flexible Combined Imager (FCI). Its 16 spectral channels measure reflected solar and emitted thermal radiation, capturing evolving cloud systems, water vapor, and atmospheric structure.

New observations provide updated initial conditions for successive AI forecast cycles.

04 · NVIDIA Earth2Studio

New observations. New forecast cycles.

The atmosphere never stops evolving. Each new set of observations provides an opportunity to initialize an updated forecast.

Using NVIDIA Earth2Studio, the workflow retrieves the latest six available Meteosat frames, prepares the model inputs, and executes StormScope-EU on NVIDIA GPUs.

Each initialization produces twelve successive predictions, extending two hours beyond the forecast reference time. As new observations become available, another forecast cycle can begin.

05 · Earthmover Arraylake + Icechunk

From GPU inference to versioned forecasts

Predictions must move efficiently from GPU compute to cloud storage.

Earthmover Arraylake receives outputs while the GPU predicts subsequent steps, overlapping transfer and inference to reduce publication latency.

After all uploads finish, one atomic Icechunk commit publishes the complete run as a consistent, versioned snapshot of multidimensional arrays.
